Framing the Problem

The real problem was the phone and the network, not the curriculum

Nigerian secondary students preparing for WAEC, NECO, and JAMB do not lack motivation or content. They lack edtech built for the devices and networks they actually have: entry-level Android phones with 1 to 3GB of RAM, shared data plans, and 2G/3G coverage that drops on the bus and at home. Most existing tools assume Western infrastructure, so they are too heavy to install, too data-hungry to open, and useless the moment the signal goes.

My point of view going in was that the hardest design constraints were not pedagogical but physical. If an app could not load in seconds on a cheap phone, stay under a few megabytes, and work fully offline, none of the adaptive-learning cleverness would ever reach the student. So the brief I set was narrow on purpose: make curriculum-aligned, adaptive exam prep that is fast and useful with no connection at all.

Research

Field research in Lagos

I interviewed 10 secondary school students, 4 teachers, and 2 school administrators across Lagos. Observed classroom dynamics, tested device capabilities, and mapped the actual learning journey.

Student interviews (n=10)

Students wanted bite-sized content for commutes, interactive quizzes that feel like games, and offline access. The #1 request: "Let me study without data."

Teacher interviews (n=4)

Teachers wanted curriculum alignment (WAEC/NECO syllabus), student progress tracking, and a supplement to classroom teaching.

Device audit (n=8)

Mix of Android 8-12, screens 5-6.5 inches, 1-3GB RAM. App must be under 15MB and load in under 2 seconds on 3G.

Competitive analysis

Analyzed uLesson, Tuteria, PrepClass, Khan Academy. Gap: none offered true offline-first with curriculum-aligned adaptive learning.

Personas

Three archetypes the research kept surfacing

These are not invented profiles. Each one is a pattern that came up repeatedly across the student, teacher, and administrator interviews, named so the team could design against a real constraint rather than an average user.

The data-rationing commuter

SS2-SS3, urban, studies in transit
"I study on the bus to school. I need something that works without data."
Pattern
Owns the phone but rations a shared data plan, so download-once-study-offline is the only model that fits her day.
Design implication
Offline-first is the product, not a feature. WiFi-time download, zero-data study.

The borrowed-device self-pacer

SS3, smaller town, evening study
"Maths is hard and my teacher moves too fast."
Pattern
Studies in short evening windows on a phone borrowed from a parent or sibling, falling behind in class on the topics that compound.
Design implication
Adaptive paths that surface prerequisites; progress that survives logging in on a shared device.

The over-stretched teacher

Classroom of 50-60, limited tech
"I have 60 students. I need to know who is falling behind."
Pattern
Wants a supplement to classroom teaching, not a replacement, and needs to spot the students slipping without grading every quiz by hand.
Design implication
Curriculum alignment to the WAEC/NECO syllabus and lightweight class-level progress visibility.
Key Design Decisions

Built for the real Nigeria

Offline-first

Lessons download over WiFi. Students study completely offline. Progress syncs when connectivity returns.

Curriculum-aligned

Every lesson maps to WAEC/NECO syllabus. Search by subject, topic, and exam year.

Adaptive paths

Quiz results adjust difficulty and recommend review topics. Struggling with quadratics? The app surfaces prerequisite algebra first.

Ultra-light UI

App under 15MB. Every screen loads in under 2 seconds on 3G. Text-first, video optional for WiFi.

Is offline a fallback mode, or the way the product works?
Chose
Offline-first, with sync on reconnect

Lessons download over WiFi and students study completely offline; progress syncs when connectivity returns. In testing, 7 of 8 students named offline access as the reason they would pick this over competitors.

Rejected
Streaming with an offline fallback

It assumes Western infrastructure. On 2G/3G that drops on the bus and at home, an app built around a live connection is useless the moment the signal goes.

Video-led lessons, or text-led?
Chose
Text-first, video optional for WiFi

It is what fits inside a 15MB app that loads in under 2 seconds on 3G, and it respects a shared, rationed data plan.

Rejected
Video as the default teaching format

Cost of the call: video is the more engaging format and the one competitors lead with. It cannot be delivered inside the device and data budgets the audit produced, so it became an extra rather than the spine.

Results & impact

Students preparing better, at scale

In production the design decisions held up where it counts: on real phones, on real networks, during real exam seasons. The offline-first, curriculum-aligned, adaptive approach turned into measurable preparation.

15k+ active students, 2M+ questions solved

The light, offline-first build let students practice on the bus and at home, not just on WiFi, which is exactly what the research asked for.

92% pass rate, +12% weekly mastery

Adaptive paths and CBT practice that mirrors the real exam moved students from rote memorisation to measured, improving preparation.

Reflections

What I learned

Design for constraints, not ideals

Great UX is not beautiful pixels on 4K screens. It is an interface that loads in 2 seconds on a phone with 1GB RAM and intermittent 3G.

Local context changes everything

Studying on a Lagos bus is fundamentally different from studying at a desk. Larger touch targets, sunlight-readable text, auto-save every 30 seconds.
